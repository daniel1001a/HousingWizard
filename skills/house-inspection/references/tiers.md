# Sorting inspection findings: examples and traps

## Worked examples (typical findings in an older house)

| Finding | Tier | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Water heater relief valve has no discharge pipe | 1 | Scald hazard the inspector flagged; cheap; licensed plumber where required |
| Open side of basement stair has no handrail | 1 | Fall hazard; cheap carpentry |
| Electrical panel of a brand insurers dislike, some dead outlets | 1 | Insurance and safety; licensed electrician and permit |
| Termite evidence at a sill | 1 | Treatment by a licensed company plus a transferable warranty; ask for prior treatment records |
| 40-year-old boiler that still works | 2 | End of life, not broken; ask for money, not a replacement |
| Roof near end of life | 2 | Same |
| Chimney mortar deterioration | 2, plus a Level 2 chimney inspection | Cause unknown; test before pricing |
| Loose cabinet doors, worn paint, overgrown shrubs | 4 | Cosmetic |
| "Monitor this crack" | 4 unless a structural engineer says otherwise | The inspector did not call it a defect |

## Traps

- **Sending the full report.** Inspectors' summaries often list only a few items; the seller will point to the summary. Send a one-page sorted list with page numbers and third-party quotes.
- **Asking the seller to fix system items.** You get the cheapest fix and no warranty.
- **Relying on a verbal "yes".** Contracts often end oral promises at closing. Every agreed item must be written into the contract, including what a price reduction replaced.
- **Asking again after the seller already moved.** A second list after a price cut risks the deal for little money. Move remaining concerns into contract protections instead.
- **Letting the tests slide past the deadline.** Where the contract has no inspection contingency, a sewer or tank problem found after signing is usually the buyer's.
- **Measurements from the inspector's scan or photos.** Inspectors rarely measure ceiling height for legal purposes. Measure yourself.

## What success looks like

Either the price lands inside the buyer's limits with the necessary items handled, or the buyer walks away having lost only the inspection fees. The one bad outcome is signing above the limits to avoid "wasting" the inspection money already spent.
