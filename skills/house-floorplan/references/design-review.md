# Designing and reviewing a proposed layout

## Ask first (answers change the design)

Ask these before drawing, use sensible defaults for anything unanswered, and list the defaults in the output so the buyer can correct them:

- Who lives here now, and who might later (a baby, parents, a caregiver)? Which rooms must be able to change use?
- Work from home: how often, how many screens, calls with the door closed?
- Pets: which, how many, and what they need on each floor (litter, food, climbing, escape risk at doors)?
- Cars: how many, which size (garage and driveway)?
- TV or no TV in the living room; guests overnight how often?
- Cooking style (high heat, heavy frying needs a stronger hood and makeup air).
- Strong likes and dislikes. Record them in the case file as preferences and apply them every round (for example: no pendant lights, indirect wall-wash lighting instead; no open shelving; nothing with its back to a stair or entry).

## Review checklist (run it before showing any proposal)

Circulation and doors
- [ ] Every door can open fully; no furniture or cabinet in its swing; closet doors not blocked.
- [ ] Main paths are at least about 3 ft wide; no path runs through the middle of a seating group.
- [ ] Furniture and appliances can physically get in: stairs, turns, door widths.

Light and outlook
- [ ] No tall storage in front of a window; the best window light goes to rooms used by day.
- [ ] Desks face or sit beside a window, not with the screen facing it.
- [ ] Seating does not have its back to a stair, entry or door where people come and go.

Flexibility
- [ ] Rooms can change use later without demolition (office that becomes a nursery; a basement room used as a workroom that can host a guest).
- [ ] The office is not placed in a room the family will soon need as a bedroom, unless the buyer wants that.

Storage
- [ ] Storage has doors unless the buyer asked for open shelves; tall storage near where things are used.
- [ ] Laundry path from bedrooms to machines considered.

Basements, attics and additions
- [ ] Bedrooms and offices below grade or in attics: legal minimum height, emergency exit and window rules where the house is. Flag, do not assume.
- [ ] New or larger exterior openings need headers and usually permits.
- [ ] Wall removals: flag for load-bearing, pipes, wires, ducts, flues.
- [ ] Wet rooms moved or added: drain slope and venting; sometimes a pump. Licensed trade.
- [ ] Openings through floors (laundry chutes, stairs): fire-stopping and joist direction.
- [ ] Kitchen hoods: makeup air above local thresholds; combustion safety with an atmospheric boiler or water heater nearby.
- [ ] Outdoor cooking and fire pits: distance from combustible walls and fences under the local fire code.

## Showing the proposal

- Always pair each proposed view with the existing view from the same camera (tab 3 of the viewer, "Save before + after image").
- Say what changed since the last version in one line per change.
- End with open questions and the flags that must be cleared by a professional before anyone quotes.
