# Reading the listing as evidence

Listing floor plans and photos are the highest-return free evidence in a purchase. They can rule out the most expensive scenario in minutes (for example: if the floor plan shows no second kitchen, the property may not need the costly path of legalizing a second unit).

## How to get them

Listing sites often block automated fetching. Use a real browser, or ask the buyer to save the photos and plan and share them. If one site blocks you, the same photo set usually appears on the brokerage's site or another portal. Never solve a CAPTCHA.

## What to look for

| Look at | Why |
|---|---|
| Floor plan totals vs recorded building area vs listing area | Disagreement suggests unrecorded additions or finished spaces |
| A second set of kitchen appliances, a separate entrance, extra meters | Features of a second dwelling unit; changes legal use and may need a certificate of occupancy |
| "Finished basement", "bonus room", "in-law", "accessory suite" | Common words for spaces that may not be legal living space |
| Roof material (slate, tile vs asphalt) | Replacement cost can differ by tens of thousands |
| Window type (steel, old single-pane vs modern) | Drives energy, lead-safe work and replacement cost |
| Heating type (steam radiators, baseboard, forced air) | Whether central air is practical, and boiler age |
| Electrical panel photo | Some brands are insurance problems (see age-risks.md) |
| Seams, rooflines and siding changes on the exterior | Where an addition meets the original house |
| Sunrooms, enclosed porches, dormers, garage conversions | Frequent unpermitted work |
| Water stains, fresh paint in one spot, new drywall in a basement corner | Possible past leaks being covered |
| Price history and relists | Real time on market and price cuts, which the current listing may hide |

## Measurements

Do not take room sizes or ceiling heights from photos or scans as facts. Listing plans are often "not to scale", and phone scans can be badly wrong on ceiling height. Use them to decide what to measure on site.
