# House age and the risks it implies (US)

Rough cost ranges are older planning estimates, not quotes. Use them to decide what to test, then replace them with local bids.

| Built | Check for | Why | Rough cost if present |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before 1978 | Lead paint | US federal disclosure rule applies; sanding, demolition and window replacement create lead dust; use lead-safe certified contractors | $8,000 to $15,000 for abatement work |
| Before about 1980 | Asbestos | Vermiculite attic insulation, 9x9 floor tiles, pipe wrap. Photograph from a distance; do not disturb | $3,000 to $20,000 |
| About 1965 to 1973 | Aluminum branch wiring | Insurers may refuse or require remediation | $5,000 to $10,000 |
| About 1950 to 1990 | Federal Pacific, Zinsco, Challenger panels | Known failure history; insurers may refuse | $2,000 to $4,000 to replace the panel |
| About 1978 to 1995 | Polybutylene (gray) supply pipe | Failure history | $8,000 to $20,000 |
| Before about 1960 | Cast iron or clay sewer line with tree roots | Collapse and blockage; the line to the street is usually the owner's responsibility | $10,000 to $30,000 |
| Any age that burned oil | Buried or abandoned oil tank | Environmental cleanup liability follows the land; usually not covered by home insurance | $20,000 to $100,000+ if leaking |
| Any | Service under 100 amps | Too small for modern loads | $3,000 to $5,000 |
| Any with a chimney, especially after a fuel change | Chimney liner and mortar | Acidic condensate from gas appliances can damage old liners | $3,000 to $6,000 for a liner |

Three checks that cost almost nothing and matter on almost every older house: sewer line camera scope, the electrical panel brand and main breaker amperage (one minute on site), and a look at insulation and pipe wrap material.
