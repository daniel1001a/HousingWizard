# Buying appliances, furniture and cabinetry

Tariff and price facts change often. Everything with a date here must be re-checked before an order.

## Timing by category (US)

| Category | Traditional best time | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Major appliances | Holiday weekends (Memorial Day, July 4, Labor Day, Black Friday); fall for washers, dryers and dishwashers | Tariffs on imported appliances and manufacturer price increases in 2025 to 2026 moved baselines up. Record today's price for the chosen model and compare at each sale; a small discount off a rising baseline may be the best there is |
| Soft goods (rugs, curtains, bedding) | January white sales, January and July clearance | Least affected by recent tariffs |
| Furniture in stock at US stores | January and July, Presidents Day for mattresses, Memorial Day for outdoor | Inventory was imported months earlier |
| Custom furniture and cabinetry from factories | Not a "wait for a sale" category | Duties are charged at customs clearance date, so schedule matters more than discounts |

Appliances usually arrive late in a renovation, after cabinets and utilities are in. Buy for the install date, not the sale date, unless storage is available and the return window covers the gap.

## Overseas custom orders: the decision rule

Landed cost is roughly: factory price, plus all duties that apply by country of origin, plus freight and insurance, plus customs brokerage, plus state sales or use tax on the total, plus local delivery. In September 2026 web research (not checked against the customs tariff schedule; re-check with a customs broker), China-origin furniture and cabinetry carried stacked duties of about 60 percent or more before any antidumping and countervailing duties on cabinets, which can be much higher for some factories; Southeast Asian origin carried less. Goods made in one country and only assembled in another are usually treated as the first country's origin.

Rules of thumb:

1. Get a real factory quote before doing any math.
2. If the quote is under half of local semi-custom pricing, landed cost may still be competitive. If it is half to two-thirds, landed cost will likely exceed local custom work, with months of waiting and warranty claims across an ocean.
3. Single pieces rarely make sense; fixed shipping and clearance costs dominate.
4. Prefer local importers who bring full containers and already handle product-safety compliance, then freight forwarders who quote delivered duty paid, before becoming the importer of record yourself.
5. No non-refundable deposits before the purchase contract is signed; prefer after clear to close so the payment does not disturb underwriting.
6. Factory holidays (Lunar New Year) shut production for weeks; orders must be confirmed well before.
