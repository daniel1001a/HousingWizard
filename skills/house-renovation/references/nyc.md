# New York City renovation rules that change the budget

Checked 2026-09. The wall, permit and license rows below were re-read on 2026-10-19 against the official pages; the dated, cited version used by the scripts is `rules/us-ny-nyc.json` in house-floorplan. Re-read current Department of Buildings rules before relying on any of this.

- **No owner exemption.** All electrical work needs a permit and a licensed master electrician. Even minor plumbing must be done by a licensed master plumber. Gas work needs a licensed plumber and a utility re-inspection. License lending is prohibited. This is stricter than much of upstate New York.
- **What owners and family may do** without a licensed trade: carpentry, framing, flooring, drywall and taping, paint, tile, cabinets, insulation, siding, decks, same-size window replacement, roof repair above the membrane. "No licensed trade" is a different question from "no permit": paint, plastering, cabinet installation and floor resurfacing are on the Department of Buildings permit-exempt list; framing, new partitions, decks and siding are not assumed to be, so confirm each with the Department.
- **Walls.** Removing a non-load-bearing wall is permit-exempt only in a Group R (residential) occupancy, only for an interior, non-fire-rated wall, and only within the smaller of 50 percent of that wall and 45 sq ft (1 RCNY 101-14, Table 2, item 2, read 2026-10-19). Removing a whole wall is more than 50 percent, so the exemption does not apply. Some districts are excluded. New partition walls are not on the exemption list. Whether a wall is load-bearing is for an engineer; no drawing or script decides it.
- **What family labor can and cannot save**: it can remove much of a contractor's labor and overhead on unlicensed work, but none of the licensed trades, abatement or legalization cost. Estimate each layer separately for each house.
- **Service upgrades** (for example 100 to 200 amps): a licensed electrician files the permit; the utility coordinates the service separately and is often the slowest part; there are inspections before energizing and at completion. A seller who "had it replaced" without the permit and sign-off has created unpermitted work.
- **Permits that touch structure, plumbing, gas or electrical, or add a bathroom** generally go through a registered architect or engineer. Allow many months; double the time if the family plans to move in only after the work.
- **Lead-safe work** is required on pre-1978 homes when disturbing paint; certified contractors cost more.
- **Kitchen exhaust**: range hoods above a certain airflow need makeup air under the mechanical code (400 CFM was the threshold read in 2026).
- **Outdoor cooking**: the fire code limits grills near combustible construction (10 ft was the figure read in 2026).
- **Property tax**: improvements raise the assessment immediately; the annual caps on one- to three-family homes do not cover physical changes.
