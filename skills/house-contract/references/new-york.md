# New York (attorney-driven market) notes

Notes checked in September 2026. Legal points are summaries to discuss with the buyer's attorney, not advice. Items marked secondhand come from practitioner blogs and need the attorney's confirmation.

## How the process runs

- An accepted offer binds no one. The seller's attorney drafts the contract of sale (often a bar-association standard form) plus a seller rider; the buyer's attorney edits and adds a buyer rider. The buyer pays the deposit (customarily 10 percent) into the seller's attorney's escrow account at signing.
- The standard contract usually has no inspection contingency. Tests happen before signing.
- The loan (mortgage) contingency is often the only exit left after signing. If the loan amount is set low relative to the price, a low appraisal may not trigger it.
- The commitment date is usually counted from the buyer's receipt of the fully signed contract, followed by a short window to give written cancellation notice. Missing the notice can cost the deposit. Put both dates in a calendar and confirm the attorney will send the notice if needed.

## Merger

Statements not written into the contract, or written without saying they survive closing, generally end at closing. So:

1. Ask seller questions in writing, and ask for the seller's own written answer, not the agent's summary.
2. Send the answer to the attorney as soon as it arrives.
3. Ask the attorney to include important answers as seller representations that survive closing.
4. A seller answer of "I don't know" protects nothing; check independently (meters, old rental ads, neighbors, complaint history).

Secondhand: fraud about an existing fact used to induce the purchase may not be cut off by merger. Ask the attorney.

## Disclosures

- The Property Condition Disclosure Statement (Real Property Law section 462) must be delivered; since March 2024 sellers can no longer opt out with a $500 credit. The 2025 form has 56 questions (checked 2026-09).
- Some sellers are exempt (section 463), for example certain fiduciaries such as executors. The seller's capacity decides whether the form applies; ask the attorney to confirm from the records.
- The form does not directly ask whether a space was ever rented. Ask in writing.
- Pre-1978 homes: federal lead paint disclosure and an inspection opportunity. Seller riders sometimes state the buyer has waived it.

## Seller rider paragraphs worth finding

Seller riders in the New York City area commonly include: as is; no representations; environmental risks (tanks, radon, asbestos) assumed by the buyer and surviving; seller may cancel if violation or certificate of occupancy costs exceed a small cap, with a list of structures the seller need not legalize; limited remedies if title cannot be cleared; buyer representations about finances; attorney fee shifting; notice addresses. Read each for the buyer's actual position.

## Engagement letters

Court rules (22 NYCRR Part 1215) require a written letter of engagement covering scope, fees, expenses and fee-dispute arbitration notice, with an exception for matters expected to cost under $3,000. Practitioner blogs listed flat fees of roughly $1,500 to $2,500 for a single-family purchase in the outer boroughs in 2026 (secondhand). Reasonable confirmations: which searches are included, whether rider negotiation is inside the flat fee, travel charges if closing is at the lender's office, and electronic copies of the deed, title policy and closing statement after closing.

## Unpermitted work and the contract

Standard clauses make the seller clear recorded violations, not unrecorded work. Unpermitted work without a violation becomes the buyer's once title passes. The practical protections are searches before signing (municipal and title), written seller answers, survival language, a price that reflects the cost of legalizing, or an escrow holdback. See house-records for the legalization framework.
