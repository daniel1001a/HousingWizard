# Contract review checklist (buyer side)

Work through every item. For each, record: where it is (document, paragraph), what it says, tier (standard, risky, do not raise), and the question for the attorney if any.

## Parties and property

- [ ] Seller names match the owner of record in the land records and tax records; all three documents spell them the same way.
- [ ] Seller's capacity stated if not an individual owner (heir, executor, trustee, attorney-in-fact). Estate or trust sales may be exempt from disclosure rules and limit what the seller can promise.
- [ ] "Seller is the sole owner" statements match the number of sellers named.
- [ ] Legal description or schedule attached; parcel ID matches.
- [ ] Included and excluded items listed (appliances, fixtures); blank "excluded" lines filled or struck.

## Money and loan

- [ ] Price, deposit amount, who holds the deposit and in what kind of account; arithmetic adds up.
- [ ] Loan amount, term, rate language, and the loan commitment date.
- [ ] How and by when the buyer must cancel if the loan is not approved; what happens if the lender withdraws after approval.
- [ ] Appraisal: whether a low appraisal gives any exit when the loan amount is fixed.
- [ ] Who pays transfer taxes, mansion or similar taxes, and recording fees.
- [ ] Credits (repairs, appliances) stated as numbers.

## Condition and repairs

- [ ] "As is" language, and what it overrides.
- [ ] Systems and appliances in working order at closing, with any exceptions listed narrowly ("only these two items").
- [ ] Every repair the seller agreed to, listed item by item, with licensed receipts, permits or warranties where relevant.
- [ ] What a price reduction replaced, written as an exception with its exact scope.
- [ ] Heat maintained until closing for a vacant house in winter.
- [ ] Broom clean, vacant at closing, no seller stay-after unless agreed.

## Waivers and risk transfers

- [ ] Inspection or due diligence period: present, waived, or absent. Where absent, all testing must happen before signing.
- [ ] Lead paint: pre-1978 homes carry a federal disclosure and an inspection opportunity (often 10 days) that contracts sometimes waive.
- [ ] Environmental risk (underground tanks, radon, asbestos, mold) moved to the buyer, and whether that survives closing.
- [ ] Seller right to cancel if violations, open permits or certificate of occupancy work cost more than a cap. Which structures are carved out.
- [ ] Buyer statements (income, no judgments): add "to the buyer's knowledge" where appropriate.

## Survival and merger

- [ ] Clause saying statements not in the contract, or not stated to survive, end at closing.
- [ ] Which seller statements survive closing. Important ones (legal use, no unpermitted work to the seller's knowledge, no rental history) are worth asking to survive; accept a no gracefully.
- [ ] Written seller answers to buyer questions are attached or incorporated.

## Title

- [ ] Title company chosen by whom; what happens if title cannot be cleared (often only the deposit and search costs are returned).
- [ ] Handwritten changes near title clauses: exact meaning of any added amount (cap or credit).
- [ ] Survey: who orders it; driveway, second structures, encroachments.

## Notices and dates

- [ ] Notice addresses and emails for both attorneys are correct and complete.
- [ ] Business days vs calendar days are consistent across documents; which document controls when they conflict.
- [ ] Closing date and place; how adjournments work and their limit (for example not past the loan commitment expiry).

## Attached and missing

- [ ] Disclosure forms attached or scheduled, or an exemption confirmed.
- [ ] Lead paint disclosure attached for pre-1978 homes.
- [ ] Any referenced schedule or exhibit actually attached.
