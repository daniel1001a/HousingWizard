# What the AI can do, and what a person must do

Show this to the buyer when they ask what they can rely on. The examples are typical cases, not guarantees.

## The AI is useful for

| Task | Example of it working |
|---|---|
| Reading long documents and sorting what matters | A long inspection report sorted into four tiers with page numbers; a contract and its riders read clause by clause |
| Cross-checking records against claims | Spotting that the seller named in a contract differs from the owner in the land records, so the attorney checks the chain of title |
| Catching what a busy professional missed | A rider clause that waives the lead paint inspection period on a pre-1978 house; a wrong email address in the notices clause |
| Building one money model | Solving cash, monthly payment and reserve together instead of one at a time, which can produce two contradictory answers |
| Explaining rules with sources | "Old" does not make unpermitted work legal; the code section that says so, and what it costs to fix |
| Drafting messages | Short, non-confrontational letters to the agent and questions to the attorney phrased as confirmations |
| Checklists for the specific house | What to measure in an old basement, which tests a house that once burned oil needs |
| Keeping one true record | A case file where old conclusions are replaced, not buried |

## The AI is not reliable for

| Task | What went wrong when it tried |
|---|---|
| Picking or ranking houses | Scraped listing data and automated scores are often stale or wrong |
| Matching an address to a record | Fuzzy matching can land on the wrong house without an error; always confirm the match |
| Measurements from scans, photos or listing plans | Phone scans can misread ceiling height by a factor of two and miss whole rooms |
| Facts from its own memory | Coordinates, rates and code sections recalled from memory can be wrong; fetch them from a source |
| Strong claims about enforcement | "The inspector will certainly write a violation" is usually more certain than the evidence allows |
| Value without real sold data | It cannot see the MLS; comparables must come from the buyer or agent |

## A person must

- **Measure** anything that decides legal use or cost: ceiling heights at the lowest point, basement depth below grade, setbacks (wall, eave and steps are three different numbers).
- **See, smell, hear and feel**: sloping floors, damp, odors, noise, street traffic, the commute at the real hour.
- **Hire licensed people** for licensed judgments: inspector, sewer scope, oil tank sweep, chimney, surveyor, structural engineer, electrician load calculation.
- **Get legal answers from their attorney**, including anything the AI said about law.
- **Decide** price, walk-away, and what to live with.
- **Send, sign and pay** everything, and confirm wiring instructions by phone.
- **Clear CAPTCHAs and logins** on public-record sites.
